# Licensing and Rights Notes

PixelForge is intended to be public and creator-friendly, but rights boundaries matter.

## Recommended default

- **Engine/source code:** MIT License.
- **Docs/templates:** MIT License unless otherwise noted.
- **Sample cartridge code:** MIT License unless otherwise noted.
- **Sample cartridge story/world/art/audio:** review before public launch. Decide whether to release as MIT, CC BY 4.0, CC BY-NC 4.0, or “all rights reserved for the sample content.”

## Why separate code and content?

The engine should be easy for people to fork and improve.

Game content is different. A demo game can teach people how to make their own without accidentally inviting someone to repackage the exact same story, characters, and brand as their own commercial release.

## Recommended public README language

> PixelForge code is MIT licensed. Demo cartridges are included for learning, playtesting, and remix experiments. Before reusing demo story, character, art, music, or brand assets in a commercial release, check the license notes for that cartridge.

## Contributor rights rule

Do not contribute assets you do not have the right to share. This includes ripped game sprites, copyrighted music, trademarked characters, AI outputs with unclear rights, and images downloaded from the web without a compatible license.

## Not legal advice

This file is a practical project boundary note, not legal advice. Run a legal review before app-store production releases or broad commercial licensing.

## External asset libraries

Third-party marketplace assets remain governed by their own licenses even when PixelForge converts or incorporates them into a project. Store purchased source files locally, do not publish standalone source packages through this MIT repository, and retain provenance/license evidence with the project.

The External Asset Forge deliberately preserves source compatibility and lane verdicts rather than treating import, export, or conversion as legal or technical approval.
